# Learner interview

Ask about the person's situation without making them complete a long intake form. Ask one question at a time and stop asking when enough is known to make a useful representative lesson.

Use short structured choices when the harness supports them. Always respect a learner's free-form answer. Do not make sensitive disclosure necessary to build a course.

## Goal and sources

Start with the goal:

> What do you want to be able to explain or do after studying this course?

Possible goals include understanding the material, preparing for an upcoming exam, applying a method, completing independent practice, or organizing a backlog of lectures.

Then establish the material:

> Which syllabus and learning materials should be treated as the core sources?

Accept files or URLs. Clarify which sources are required, optional, current, or historical. Ask for missing materials that materially change the result; do not repeatedly ask for files already supplied.

When an assessment is relevant:

> What is the next assessment, and where are its official scope and instructions?

Do not ask the learner to provide actual exam questions. Preserve the exact timezone of deadlines. Unknown assessment details remain unknown.

## Age, optionally

Ask once:

> Would you like to share your age or an age band to help set appropriate context and language? You can skip this.

Suggested choices:

- Under 13
- 13–15
- 16–17
- 18–24
- 25 or older
- Prefer not to say

If the learner prefers an exact age, accept it without requesting a birthdate. If an age band is supplied, do not infer an exact number. If skipped, leave age absent.

The released optional private profile uses `ageBand` values `under-13`,
`13-15`, `16-17`, `18-24`, `25-plus`, or `prefer-not-to-say`. Convert an
explicitly supplied age to the appropriate band rather than retaining a
birthdate or exact age. If skipped, use `prefer-not-to-say` or do not create a
profile. Never place the band in Course Pack metadata.

Use the answer only where useful for appropriate language, examples, support, and safety. Do not treat it as evidence of intelligence, prerequisite knowledge, or qualification.

Never add age to the shareable course. Do not include the answer in public generation metadata, source filenames, export URLs, analytics, or logs.

For learners under 18, avoid requesting names, birthdates, location, account credentials, or medical documents. Keep this workflow local and avoid encouraging unapproved sharing of school material. Do not claim compliance with any child-privacy law merely because the app uses local storage.

## Course level

Ask:

> Should I use the course's stated level, infer a provisional level from the syllabus, or let you choose?

When the learner chooses:

- Middle school
- High school
- Bachelor's / undergraduate
- Master's / graduate
- Another level or independent study
- Not sure

These labels describe the course, not the learner's ability.

### Evidence for optional inference

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. An explicit course or syllabus statement.
2. Institutional catalog designation.
3. Required prerequisites and intended learning outcomes.
4. Source complexity, with significant uncertainty.

Do not infer level from:

- Age or appearance.
- A diagnostic label.
- A single difficult paragraph.
- A course-code number without institution-specific evidence.
- The learner's job title alone.

Report the result plainly:

> The syllabus labels this a graduate course and lists these prerequisites. I propose master's level for the course, with a foundational explanation where needed. You can override that.

If no evidence supports a choice, use unknown. If sources disagree, expose the disagreement. A middle-school course studied by an adult remains a middle-school course; the presentation can still respect the adult's experience.

## Prior knowledge and scaffolding

Ask:

> What relevant background do you already have, and which part feels hardest?

Use background to make helpful analogies without substituting them for standard vocabulary.

Suggested scaffolding choices:

- Start from foundations.
- Explain the mechanism, with worked examples.
- Go deeper into assumptions, formal reasoning, and counterexamples.

A senior engineer may be comfortable with code but unfamiliar with a concept's formal or cognitive-science meaning. Do not skip the explanation because the person can program.

## Presentation barriers and preferences

Ask:

> What gets in the way of learning from your current materials?

Possible choices:

- Too much on screen.
- Hard to find where I left off.
- Unfamiliar vocabulary.
- Not enough examples or diagrams.
- Difficulty starting or switching tasks.

Offer practical preferences, not fixed learning-style categories:

- One step at a time or full chapter reading.
- Diagram plus text, not diagram instead of text.
- Short utility labels and clear source terms.
- Optional notes and thought parking.
- More prerequisite explanation.

Do not require disclosure of ADHD, dyslexia, anxiety, disability, or medical history. The learner can state the desired adjustment without explaining why.

## Time, optionally

Ask:

> Do you want time tracking or study timers, or should learning stay untimed?

Suggested choices:

- Untimed, with no break cues.
- Optional time tracking, hidden until I use it.
- A visible, pauseable study timer.

Study timing and an assessment's official timed format are separate settings. Never turn on study countdowns because the syllabus includes a timed exam.

If a weekly plan is wanted, ask for the available budget separately. Estimates are planning suggestions, not instructor workload requirements or a promise of completion.

## Private profile consent

Before writing personal answers to a file:

> Should I save these preferences in a separate private profile, or use them only for this generation?

Default to no private profile when consent is absent. Either choice must still allow generating the course.

## Use feedback to improve

After the representative lesson:

> Does this explanation connect clearly to your course, and is the depth appropriate?

Ask for one useful correction. A request for less visual clutter should not be interpreted as a request to remove explanatory depth.
